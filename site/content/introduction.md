# Introduction

A kata is a small prepared response to a moment that keeps showing up at work. Each one names a trigger, such as a ticket landing for refinement or a plan you are about to commit to, and gives you a few steps to follow when it arrives. It takes 10 to 15 minutes and ends with something written down.

## Where They Came From

About 20 years ago I broke production, and for the few hours the rollback took I was sure the company would close because of me. A few days later, someone on the team told me to anticipate failure and write down what might go wrong before it did. The advice took a few more incidents to stick, and the list I started keeping before risky changes eventually became the Pre-Mortem Minute.

The other katas started the same way, as habits picked up over the years. They stayed habits until March 2026, when training the next group of tech leads at Epignosis meant turning them into something I could hand over.

## Practicing a Kata

You need no team, coach, or permission, and you do not need to be a tech lead yet. Pick one kata and stay with it for a week, keeping a short note as you go with 3 questions:

- Did the moment arise?
- What did I do?
- What would I change?

A week in which the moment never came up still tells you something. After that, move on to the next kata.

## What to Expect

I make no promises about results. Some of these katas have worked for me and for people I passed them to over the years. Others that worked for me did not land for someone else, and I have abandoned a few myself.
